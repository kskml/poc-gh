6Formula Reference — Nothing Hidden

▾

Every displayed number comes from one of these formulas. Symbols: FTEB baseline headcount · SP sprint story points · s sprints/month · L labor €/FTE/month · A AI seat €/FTE-month · m industry multiplier · g the gain in question (observed g or adjusted gadj).

F1  story pts/FTE/sprint  = SP ÷ FTE
F2  monthly output O  = SPbaseline × s
F3  monthly cost C  = FTEB × L
F4  productivity Prod  = O ÷ FTEB  (story pts/FTE/month)
F5  cost per story point CP  = C ÷ O
F6  observed gain g  = (SPw÷FTEw) ÷ (SPB÷FTEB) − 1  Phase 1
F7  adjusted gain gadj  = g + m  additive — Phase 2
F8  FTE-months needed  = FTEB ÷ (1+g)
F9  capacity released  = FTEB − FTE needed  = FTEB × g ÷ (1+g)
F10  capacity value  = capacity released × L
F11  AI tool spend  = FTE-months needed × A
F12  monthly cost reduction S  = C − FTE needed × (L+A)  = capacity value − AI spend
F13  break-even gain gbe  = (L+A) ÷ L − 1
F14  tool-parity multiplier  = gbe − g  multiplier at which S = 0
F15  combined % gain  = Σ(FTEi × gi) ÷ ΣFTEi  FTE-weighted
F16  combined €/story point  = Σ(Oi × CPi) ÷ ΣOi  output-weighted

Worked example (Team 1, defaults): O = 58.6 × 2 = 117.2 story points · C = 6 × €1,200 = €7,200 · g = (56.4÷5.5) ÷ (58.6÷6) − 1 = +4.99% · FTE needed = 6 ÷ 1.0499 = 5.71 · capacity released = 0.29 FTE, value = 0.29 × €1,200 = €343 · AI spend = 5.71 × €100 = €571 · S = 7,200 − 5.71 × €1,300 = −€229 (a cost increase). Phase 2 at m = +8.69%: gadj = +13.69% → FTE needed = 5.28 → S = 7,200 − 5.28 × €1,300 = +€339.

7Assumptions

▾

Key assumptions A1–A10

  • A1 · Cadence: 2 sprints/month per team, stable sprint composition; no vacation or holiday effects modelled.
  • A2 · Labor: €1,200 per FTE per month, fully loaded, same in both states.
  • A3 · AI tool: €100 per FTE-month for every active FTE-month; loaded AI-enabled FTE-month = €1,300.
  • A4 · Same-output framing: scenarios deliver the baseline monthly output at adjusted efficiency; freed capacity is redeployed, not a headcount cut.
  • A5 · Additive multiplier: adjusted gain = observed + multiplier. Compounding would add only ~0.5–1.0 pt at these values; additive is the conservative choice.
  • A6 · Rounding & combined rows: per-team euros rounded, combined = exact sum of rounded parts; combined % = FTE-weighted; combined €/story point = output-weighted.
  • A7 · Confounding: the with-AI sprint bundles AI with concurrent staffing changes (Team 1 −0.5, Team 2 +2.0 FTE); per-FTE normalization controls for size but cannot separate the effects.
  • A8 · Evidence depth: one sprint per team per state — treat observed results as directions, not rates, until a rolling multi-sprint baseline confirms them.
  • A9 · Multipliers are benchmarks: Accenture × GitHub, Bain, Gartner and Capgemini figures are external planning evidence, not measurements of these two teams; the observed rows are the only measured data here.
  • A10 · Currency: figures display in euro (€); the arithmetic is currency-agnostic.

Limitations. Story points are an output proxy, not a value measure; one sprint per state is thin evidence; the staffing changes confound the with-AI observation; industry multipliers are external benchmarks; and the model is linear per month — it does not simulate ramp-up, quality rework, or scope mix. Use the observed rows for "what happened", the Phase 2 rows for "what a disciplined program should beat", and re-baseline every sprint.

What improves productivity & cost reduction most in a disciplined pipeline

Ranked by how much they move the two outcomes. These are the levers that turn the Phase 1 result into the Phase 2 multiplier.

1Route work to where AI is strongestSegment tasks: greenfield code (35–55%), unit tests, documentation (45–50%), review prep. DORA's reliance table shows exactly where teams already lean on AI — route deliberately, not by habit.
2Make AI review a pipeline gateDuolingo cut review turnaround 3h → 1h; DeputyDev measured −23% review time per PR. Review is the highest-leverage shared stage — automate it first.
3Generate tests as a CI stageMeta's TestGen-LLM saw 73% of AI tests accepted; GitHub's study found 53.2% higher odds of passing all unit tests. Tests convert AI speed into shippable speed and cut the 45% "debugging AI code" tax.
4Standardize prompts & playbooksZiegler et al. (CACM): acceptance rate is the strongest predictor of realized productivity. Templates and prompt libraries raise acceptance and cut variance across developers.
5Onboard deliberately; respect the expert trapNewcomers gain most (+25% at Duolingo); experts on mature code can go backwards (METR −19%). Assign AI-heavy flow to unfamiliar/greenfield work; keep experts on review and routing.
6Redesign the process, not just the toolDORA 2024: more AI adoption without delivery redesign slightly reduced throughput (−1.5%). The multiplier comes from changing the pipeline, not from buying seats.
7Measure unit economics monthlyTrack story points/FTE and € per story point per sprint (this tool's F4/F5). METR's perception gap (felt +20%, measured −19%) only surfaces with unit metrics.
8Beat the measured floor deliberatelyThe +8.69% Accenture × GitHub RCT figure is this tool's default multiplier; treat it as the floor to beat. Route work, gate reviews, generate tests, standardize prompts — and re-baseline every sprint.
