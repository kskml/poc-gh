2What “Productivity Gain %” actually means

Time saved is not productivity gained — the two are linked by a formula. If AI cuts the time of a task by 20% (5 days → 4 days), you do not gain 20% more output: in the 20 days that used to fit 4 tasks you now finish 5 — a +25% gain. Productivity is output per hour, so removing 20% of the hours raises output by 1/(1−0.20) − 1 = +25%.

r (time cut) = g/(1+g)   ·   g (output gain) = 1/(1−r) − 1 = r/(1−r)

3Estimated tokens per FTE — the fuel gauge

AI usage is measured in tokens (roughly ¾ of an English word each). Your team’s user-story evidence measures 3.5M tokens per story point delivered (Sonnet-5-class agentic coding) — a 5-point story carries ~17.5M. Laid over the anchor, one FTE-month is a single multiplication:

Why the token estimate is so useful

  1. It turns the known Y into a per-token price. You already know Y — dividing it by the measured tokens gives the effective price you actually pay per million tokens.
  2. It is the earliest adoption meter. Tokens are objective. If a developer’s monthly usage is far below ~90M, the modeled gain is not materializing for them: no tokens, no savings. Check tokens before believing any g (Langfuse / LiteLLM / Helicone can attribute per user).
  3. It prices a saved hour. An AI-assisted saved hour costs a fraction of a normal billed hour.
  4. It is the budget line. — the AI spend the business case below consumes.
